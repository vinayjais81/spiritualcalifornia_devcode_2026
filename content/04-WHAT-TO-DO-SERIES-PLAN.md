# Spiritual California -- "What To Do" Series

A second content line: situation-first self-help articles. Companion to the 50-article modality
journal, not a replacement for it.

---

## 1. Why situation-first

The journal answers **"what is this modality?"** The reader has to already know what they want.

This line answers **"what do I do about this?"** The reader arrives with a problem, not a
vocabulary. That is the more common state, the higher-volume search behaviour, and the more
urgent moment.

**Strategic function:** every article in this series routes into one or two journal articles.
Fifty modality pieces are a reference library nobody browses. This is the front door that makes
them get read.

**Evidence advantage:** every technique below comes from the free, well-evidenced tier already
researched for the journal. Competitors' self-help content cites nothing. Ours cites primary
literature, in a footer, without lecturing.

## 2. Format spec -- deliberately different from the journal

| | Journal | What To Do |
|---|---|---|
| Length | 1,100-1,500 words | **600-900 words** |
| Opens with | A scene, then evidence | **The technique, in the first 100 words** |
| Evidence | The body of the piece | **A footer box, 3-4 sentences** |
| Tone | Editorial, analytical | **Direct, practical, warm** |
| Structure | 8 beats | **5 beats** |
| Reading context | Considered, at a desk | **On a phone, mid-problem** |

### The five beats

1. **The situation, in two sentences.** Named precisely enough that the right reader recognizes
   themselves. No preamble.
2. **Do this now.** One primary technique. Numbered steps. Time-boxed and specific --
   "90 seconds," "four days," "tonight." No more than six steps.
3. **If that doesn't fit.** Two alternates, one line each. Different people, different
   constraints.
4. **Why it works.** 3-4 sentences. Named study, linked, effect stated honestly and small when
   small.
5. **When this isn't enough.** Non-negotiable in every article. What would indicate the problem
   needs a clinician, a doctor, or a practitioner rather than a technique.

### Front matter additions

Reuse the journal schema, plus:

```yaml
series:        "what-to-do"
situation:     "Waking at 3am and unable to stop thinking"   # R
timeToTry:     "90 seconds"                                   # R
primaryTechnique: "slow-breathing"                            # R -- for cross-linking
routesTo:      ["slow-breathing-nervous-system", "yoga-nidra-non-sleep-rest"]  # R
escalation:    "clinician"   # R -- one of: none | practitioner | clinician | urgent
```

`escalation` drives a template component. `urgent` renders crisis resources at the top, not the
bottom.

## 3. The evidence toolkit

Every article draws its primary technique from this set. All free, all researched already.

| Technique | Evidence standing | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| **Slow breathing, ~6/min, 5 min** | Meta-analytic; no device needed; longer exhale helps | Acute arousal, before hard moments |
| **Morning daylight, 15-30 min** | Tier A mechanism; APA-recommended for SAD | Sleep timing, winter mood, drift |
| **Awe walk, 15 min weekly** | Randomized trial in older adults | Narrowed attention, self-preoccupation |
| **Expressive writing, 4 days x 15 min** | Small effect (d ≈ 0.16), context-dependent | Unresolved events you can't stop replaying |
| **Walking, 20-30 min** | Large pooled effect, but only 41% of trials beat control | Low mood, rumination, decision fatigue |
| **Water** | Mood and vigilance decline below 1% body water loss | Afternoon fog, headache, irritability |
| **Ten taught minutes of mindfulness** | Dose research: duration didn't predict outcome; teaching did | Anyone who has failed at 45 minutes |
| **Body scan / yoga nidra** | Thin evidence, very low risk, no effort required | Too tired to do anything active |
| **Loving-kindness, 10 min** | Moves interpersonal attitudes; does *not* move life satisfaction | Feeling hardened toward someone |
| **Doing something rhythmic with other people** | The group appears to be the mechanism, not the drum | Isolation, flatness |

**Rule:** never claim more than the tier supports. "This has a small effect and costs nothing"
is a better sentence than any overstatement, and it is the series' differentiator.

## 5. The safety boundary -- read this before commissioning anything

**These situations must never receive a technique article.** No breathing exercise, no journaling
prompt, no practice.

- Suicidal thoughts or self-harm
- Disordered eating
- Active abuse or violence at home
- Acute psychosis or mania
- Substance withdrawal
- Any acute medical emergency

They get **one page**: a plainly designed resources page with current crisis lines, no
techniques, no modalities, no CTA, and no advertising of any practitioner. It should be
reachable from every article footer in the series.

**Articles requiring specific care in drafting:**

- **A2 (chest tight, racing thoughts).** Breath-focused technique is defensible here, but our own
  breathwork research found that attending to respiration triggers panic in some people --
  particularly those with panic history. The article must say so and offer a non-breath
  alternative. Do not describe panic symptoms in vivid detail.
- **C1, C2, C3 (sleep).** Must state that persistent insomnia has medical causes -- apnoea,
  thyroid, medication, alcohol, depression -- and that CBT-I is the first-line treatment, not a
  relaxation practice. Our yoga nidra research is explicit on this.
- **D4, D5, D7 (medical waiting, grief, breakup).** Expressive writing makes most people feel
  *worse* immediately, with benefit appearing over weeks. Say so. Do not recommend it to someone
  in acute grief without the caution.
- **E2, E3, E5 (physical symptoms).** Every one must name the red flags that mean see a doctor
  rather than try a technique.

**A rule for the whole series:** the "When this isn't enough" section is never optional and never
softened. It is what separates responsible self-help from the genre's default.

## 6. Sequencing

**Phase 1 -- 8 articles, launch.** A1, A2, B1, C1, C2, E4, G2, G5.
Highest search volume, all built on the strongest evidence, all free techniques. Establishes the
series' voice and the escalation pattern before anything harder ships.

**Phase 2 -- 12 articles.** Complete Clusters A and B, add C3-C6.
Working-day content is the most shareable and the closest fit to your audience.

**Phase 3 -- 16 articles.** Clusters D, E, F, G.
These are longer, harder, and carry more escalation. Ship them once the safety template has been
exercised on easier material.

**Cadence:** two per week alongside the journal's two per week is too much. Alternate — journal
one week, What To Do the next. Twenty weeks to complete Phase 1 and 2.

## 7. Two things to decide before writing starts

**1. Does this series get its own visual identity?** Same palette and type, but the illustration
brief should differ -- the journal's images are contemplative still lifes. These should be more
diagrammatic and immediate. Consider a simple time-badge on each hero image ("90 seconds," "4
days") as a recurring device. That badge is the series' visual signature and it does real work
for the reader.

**2. Does the evidence-tier badge appear here too?** Our recommendation: **no.** The tier badge
is the journal's device and works because those articles are *about* evidence. Here it would
clutter a page whose job is to get someone to do something in the next ninety seconds. Keep the
footer citation; drop the badge.
