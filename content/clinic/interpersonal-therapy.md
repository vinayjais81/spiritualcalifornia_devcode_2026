CBT asks what you are thinking. Behavioural activation asks what you are doing. Interpersonal
Therapy asks a third question, and for a lot of people it is the one that fits:

**What has changed in your relationships, and when did the depression start?**

It is recommended in practice guidelines as one of the two psychological treatments of choice for
depression — the other being CBT — and almost nobody outside the field has heard of it.

## What it actually is

IPT is a structured, time-limited therapy, usually twelve to sixteen sessions, developed
specifically for major depression. Its premise is not that relationships cause depression. It is
that depression occurs in an interpersonal context, and that changing that context reliably
changes the depression.

After the assessment, the work is organized around **one of four problem areas**:

- **Grief** — depression following a death, especially where mourning stalled
- **Role transition** — a change in life role: new parenthood, redundancy, retirement, illness,
  divorce, migration
- **Role dispute** — an ongoing conflict with a specific person where expectations have diverged
- **Interpersonal deficits** — long-standing difficulty forming or sustaining connection, used
  when none of the other three fit

You pick one. The therapy then works on that.

The other distinctive feature: IPT explicitly treats depression as a **medical illness** you are
not to blame for. That framing is deliberate, and for people whose depression comes packaged with
self-blame, it is often the most therapeutic thing in the first session.

## What the research shows

### The depression evidence

Cuijpers and colleagues meta-analyzed 38 studies covering 4,356 patients. Against standard or no
treatment, IPT produced
[**d = 0.63 (95% CI 0.36 to 0.90), with a number needed to treat of 2.91**](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK85991/)
across 16 RCTs, with no evidence of publication bias.

An NNT of about three is a genuinely strong figure: treat three people, and one benefits who
otherwise would not have.

The larger and more recent synthesis covered **90 studies and 11,434 participants** across all
mental health problems. For acute-phase depression, IPT showed
[**moderate-to-large effects against control groups, g = 0.60 (95% CI 0.45 to
0.75)**](https://research.vu.nl/en/publications/interpersonal-psychotherapy-for-mental-health-problems-a-comprehe/).

### And then the dodo bird again

In that same analysis: **no significant difference against other therapies (differential g = 0.06)
and none against pharmacotherapy (g = −0.13)**.

That is the pattern this whole series keeps meeting. IPT beats doing nothing by a clear margin,
and ties with everything else that is properly delivered — including medication. It is worth
noting that Cuijpers, who produced these figures, is also the author of the sharpest critique of
the [common factors position](/clinic/common-factors-dodo-bird). He is not grinding an axe.

### Beyond depression

- **Anxiety disorders:** large effects against control groups, with
  [no evidence that IPT is less effective than CBT](https://psychiatryonline.org/doi/10.1176/appi.ajp.2015.15091141).
- **Eating disorders:** significant effects, though probably slightly smaller than CBT in the
  acute phase.
- **Relapse prevention:** combination maintenance treatment with medication plus IPT outperformed
  medication alone at preventing relapse — odds ratio 0.37 (95% CI 0.19 to 0.73), NNT 7.63.

### One practical finding

Trials using **manuals adapted to the specific population** produced significantly larger effects
than those using the standard manual unmodified — d = 0.67 versus 0.29 (p = 0.004). IPT appears
to reward being tailored, which is one reason it has been successfully adapted for adolescents,
perinatal depression, and low-resource settings worldwide.

**The honest limitation.** Only 9 of the 38 studies in the 2011 analysis met all quality criteria
— 24%. The authors note this is actually high for this literature, where they had previously found
only 10% of depression psychotherapy trials met all criteria. High relative to a low bar is still
a caveat. Risk of bias was present in the majority of studies in the larger analysis too, though
there was little indication it changed the outcome.

**Where the evidence stands.** Guideline-recommended treatment of choice, large evidence base, consistent effects
against controls, comparable to CBT and to medication.

## What it does not do

**It is not couples therapy or family therapy.** IPT is individual. Other people are discussed,
not usually present.

**It does not fix the relationship.** In a role dispute, a legitimate outcome is deciding the
relationship cannot be renegotiated and grieving that. The target is your depression, not the
other person's behaviour.

**It is not better than the alternatives.** See the differential effect sizes. This is a genuine
choice among equals, and the reason to choose it is fit.

**And it is not indefinite.** Twelve to sixteen sessions, with the ending discussed from the
start. If you want open-ended relational work, that is psychodynamic therapy, not IPT.

## What the sessions actually involve

**Sessions 1-3.** Assessment of depressive symptoms, then the **interpersonal inventory** — a
systematic review of your significant relationships, current and past, what you expect from each,
what you get. Then linking the depression to a timeline, and jointly choosing the problem area.
You leave the initial phase with an explicit formulation and a focus.

**Sessions 4-9.** Work on the chosen area with techniques matched to it. In grief work, that means
facilitating mourning that stalled and rebuilding other relationships. In a role dispute, mapping
what each party expects and where those diverged, then communication analysis — reconstructing a
specific conversation line by line to see where it went wrong. In role transition, mourning the
lost role while building the new one.

**Sessions 10-12+.** Consolidation, anticipating future difficulty, and explicitly ending. The
ending is treated as a role transition in itself and worked with.

Homework exists but is lighter than CBT — usually noticing, or attempting a specific conversation.

## Is this available to me?

**This is IPT's real weakness in the US.** It is far less widely trained than CBT, and many
therapists have never delivered it, despite its guideline status.

**Where to look:** the International Society of Interpersonal Psychotherapy maintains information
on trained clinicians. Academic medical centres and university clinics are more likely to have IPT
therapists than private practice.

**Adaptations are widespread internationally**, particularly IPT for perinatal depression and for
adolescents, so maternal health services and adolescent services are worth asking.

Billed as psychotherapy.

## How to ask for it

> *"I'd like to try interpersonal therapy for depression. My depression started around [a
> bereavement / a major life change / an ongoing conflict], and IPT is guideline-recommended
> alongside CBT. Do you have anyone trained in it?"*

Naming the trigger matters, because it tells the clinician which of the four problem areas you
would likely work in — and if IPT is unavailable, it still points them toward the right focus in
whatever therapy you do get.

## Who this suits

On the evidence and the structure, IPT is worth asking for specifically if:

- Your depression **started around an identifiable event** — a death, a birth, a job loss, a move,
  a diagnosis, a separation
- You are **stuck in a specific unresolved conflict**
- You find thought records alienating, and the honest description of your problem is
  circumstantial rather than cognitive
- **Grief has not moved.** IPT's grief work is one of its strongest applications

It suits less well someone whose depression has no discernible interpersonal shape, or who wants
a technique-heavy, homework-heavy structure — for whom [CBT](/clinic/what-cbt-actually-is) or
[behavioural activation](/clinic/behavioural-activation) may fit better.

## When this isn't enough

If twelve to sixteen sessions produce nothing, the alternatives carry equivalent evidence and
switching is normal. Given the differential effect sizes above, no one modality has a claim to be
the last resort.

Medication is a legitimate parallel option — IPT matched pharmacotherapy in the pooled analyses,
and the combination outperformed medication alone for relapse prevention.

And if the depression is severe, or accompanied by thoughts of harming yourself, that needs a
clinician now rather than a referral pathway. Contact your doctor or emergency services.
