Psilocybin is closer to approval than any classic psychedelic has ever been, and the gap between
what the research shows and what the coverage implies is unusually wide.

## What it actually is

Psilocybin-assisted therapy is not a drug taken at home. In the trial protocols it means a single
supervised dosing session — typically six to eight hours, in a prepared room, with two trained
monitors present — bracketed by preparation sessions beforehand and integration sessions
afterwards.

**COMP360** is Compass Pathways' synthetic psilocybin formulation, and it is the compound furthest
through the regulatory process.

The therapy is not the drug alone. The protocol, the monitors and the integration sessions are part
of what was tested, and any future approval would almost certainly carry a REMS-style requirement
for supervised administration — as
[esketamine does](/clinic/ketamine-and-esketamine).

## Where the regulation actually stands

**The clinical results.** COMP005, reported June 2025, was the
[first positive Phase 3 result for any classic psychedelic in any psychiatric indication in US
history](https://www.medicaldaily.com/psilocybin-fda-approval-2026-compass-pathways-comp360-treatment-resistant-depression-475822).
COMP006 confirmed it in February 2026, and positive 26-week durability data followed on
[7 July 2026](https://psychedelicbeacon.com/blog/psilocybin-fda-approval-tracker).

**The regulatory sequence.** On 18 April 2026, an executive order directed federal agencies to
accelerate review of psychedelic treatments. Six days later, on 24 April, the FDA issued
**National Priority Vouchers** to three developers — Compass for COMP360 in treatment-resistant
depression, the Usona Institute for psilocybin in major depressive disorder, and Transcend
Therapeutics for methylone in PTSD. The voucher
[compresses the standard 10-to-12-month review to roughly one to two
months](https://www.medicaldaily.com/psilocybin-fda-priority-review-2026-compass-pathways-depression-ptsd-approval-475666)
after filing.

The FDA also granted Compass a **rolling review**, allowing sections of the application to be
submitted as completed. Sections have been submitted; the final NDA is targeted for Q4 2026, with a
decision plausible in late 2026 or early 2027.

**And the political detail worth knowing.** In February 2026, STAT News reported that senior
administration officials had removed COMP360 from the priority voucher programme hours before its
announcement — psilocybin was the only one of ten proposed drugs vetoed. That was reversed in
April. We include it because it illustrates how contingent this timeline is on politics rather than
data.

**State programmes are a separate thing.** Oregon (Measure 109) and Colorado (Proposition 122)
operate licensed psilocybin service centres under state law, in
[direct conflict with federal law](https://vicentellp.com/insights/comp360-rems-and-what-fda-psychedelic-approval-path-means-for-investors-and-operators/).
These are **not** FDA-approved medical treatment, they are not covered by insurance, and the
practitioners are facilitators rather than licensed clinicians in most cases.

## What the research actually shows

Here is the number that appears less often than it should.

In the COMP006 trial, the 25mg dose met its primary endpoint with a
[**3.8-point reduction on the MADRS from baseline compared with a 1mg control
dose**](https://www.clinicaltrialsarena.com/news/compass-pathways-psychedelic-comp360-trd-phase-iii/).

The MADRS runs 0-60. A 3.8-point difference is statistically significant with high confidence and
**clinically modest** — the threshold usually cited for a clinically meaningful difference on that
scale is around 2 points, so it clears the bar without clearing it by much.

That is a real result for treatment-resistant depression, where options are limited. It is also not
the transformation the popular coverage describes.

### The functional unblinding problem

This is the methodological issue that dominates the field, and in July 2026 the FDA finalized
guidance specifically addressing it.

**Functional unblinding** occurs when participants in a supposedly double-blind trial can
accurately identify whether they received the active drug — which, with psilocybin, they almost
always can, because the perceptual effects are unmistakable.
[When blinding fails, participants who know they received the active drug expect to benefit, and
those who did not expect not to, biasing reported
outcomes](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/320454/20260714/fda-finalizes-psychedelic-trial-rules-unblinding-problem-gets-official-answer.htm).

It is the issue that
[derailed Lykos Therapeutics' MDMA application in 2024](/clinic/mdma-assisted-therapy), and
Compass's trial design — using a 1mg active control rather than placebo — is an attempt to address
it.

**Readers of this journal will recognise the problem.** It is the same one we have flagged across
fifty wellness modalities: you cannot conceal the intervention, so expectancy contaminates the
result. The difference is that psychedelic research has an FDA guidance document about it, and
acupuncture does not.

**Where the evidence stands.** Two positive Phase 3 trials with durability data — genuinely
unprecedented for this class — showing a statistically robust and clinically modest effect, in a
field where the central methodological problem is acknowledged but not solved.

## What it does not do

**It is not approved, and it is not legally available as medical treatment in the US** as of the
date above.

**It is not a single-session cure.** The trials involve preparation, supervised dosing and
integration, and the durability data extends to 26 weeks — not indefinitely.

**It is not what is sold at retreats.** Underground and overseas psilocybin retreats are not
delivering the trial protocol, do not have medical screening, and have no adverse-event reporting.

**And the risks are real.** Psilocybin can precipitate prolonged psychological difficulty,
particularly in people with a personal or family history of psychosis or bipolar disorder — which
is why trials screen those groups out. Cardiovascular effects, acute anxiety and confusion during
dosing, and lasting distress afterwards are all documented. The supervised setting exists for
reasons.

## What a session involves, in the trials

**Preparation:** several hours across multiple sessions with the therapists who will be present,
establishing what to expect and what to do if it becomes frightening.

**Dosing:** six to eight hours, lying down, eyeshades, curated music, two monitors present
throughout. Participants are not left alone at any point. Effects typically begin within 30-60
minutes and peak around two hours.

**Integration:** sessions in the days and weeks afterwards, working with what came up. Practitioners
widely regard this as where the therapeutic work happens, though it has not been isolated
experimentally.

## What to do with this information

**If you have treatment-resistant depression now**, the available options are covered elsewhere in
this series: [esketamine](/clinic/ketamine-and-esketamine), which is FDA-approved, insured and
available today; [rTMS](/clinic/rtms-depression); medication combinations; and ECT, which despite
its reputation remains the most effective treatment for severe treatment-resistant depression.

**If you want to access psilocybin legally**, the routes as of this date are clinical trials
(searchable on ClinicalTrials.gov), FDA Expanded Access in limited circumstances, and the Oregon and
Colorado state programmes — with the caveat that the last of these are not medical treatment and
operate in conflict with federal law.

**If you are considering an underground or overseas option**, the things to know: no screening for
the psychiatric contraindications that trials exclude, no medical support if something goes wrong,
no regulation of who is in the room, and no recourse. The trial safety record does not transfer to
settings without the trial's safeguards.

**And do not stop your current treatment** in anticipation of something that is not yet approved.

## When this isn't enough

If you are severely depressed and waiting for a treatment that may or may not arrive, that wait is
itself a risk. The treatments available now have real evidence, and our
[article on finding a therapist](/clinic/finding-a-therapist) covers how to access them.

**If you are in crisis or having thoughts of harming yourself**, contact your doctor, a crisis
line, or emergency services today.
