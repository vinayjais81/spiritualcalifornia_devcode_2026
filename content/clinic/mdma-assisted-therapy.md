For several years, MDMA-assisted therapy was the treatment most likely to become the first
approved psychedelic medicine. Trial results were strong, coverage was extensive, and approval was
widely expected in 2024.

**In August 2024 the FDA declined the application** and asked for an additional Phase 3 trial.

The reasons are the most instructive thing in this article, and they matter well beyond MDMA.

## What it actually is

In the trial protocols, MDMA-assisted therapy is not medication. It is a small number of supervised
sessions — typically three, each lasting six to eight hours, with two therapists present throughout
— surrounded by preparation and integration sessions.

The proposed mechanism is that MDMA reduces fear response and increases the tolerability of
approaching traumatic material, allowing therapeutic work that would otherwise be too distressing.
That is a plausible complement to what the evidence-based trauma treatments already do —
[prolonged exposure](/clinic/prolonged-exposure-ptsd) fails for many people precisely because it is
intolerable.

## Where the regulation actually stands

**The rejection.** The FDA declined Lykos Therapeutics' New Drug Application for PTSD in
[August 2024, requesting an additional Phase 3
trial](https://mindmedicinelaw.com/tools/legal-status-by-state).

**The status now.** MDMA remains Schedule I in the US. Access is limited to clinical trials and FDA
Expanded Access in restricted circumstances.

**Australia is the exception.** Since July 2023, Australia is
[the only country where MDMA can be legally prescribed](https://mindmedicinelaw.com/tools/legal-status-by-state),
for PTSD, under the Therapeutic Goods Administration's Authorised Prescriber scheme.

**And the field has moved sideways.** Transcend Therapeutics is developing **methylone (TSND-201)**
for PTSD — a synthetic compound similar to MDMA but with a distinct chemical profile,
[designed to address the regulatory and ethical concerns that led to the 2024
rejection](https://www.medicaldaily.com/psilocybin-fda-priority-review-2026-compass-pathways-depression-ptsd-approval-475666).
It received a National Priority Voucher in April 2026 alongside two psilocybin developers.

Meanwhile [psilocybin](/clinic/psilocybin-assisted-therapy) has overtaken MDMA as the classic
psychedelic closest to approval.

## Why the application failed

This is the part worth reading carefully, because the issues are not exotic.

**Functional unblinding.** In a supposedly double-blind trial, participants who receive MDMA can
tell. The subjective effects are unmistakable.
[When blinding fails, participants who know they received the active drug expect to benefit and
those who did not expect not to, biasing reported
outcomes](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/320454/20260714/fda-finalizes-psychedelic-trial-rules-unblinding-problem-gets-official-answer.htm)
— and the primary outcome in PTSD trials is a symptom rating, not a blood test.

The FDA cited concerns about
[trial design and the difficulty of blinding in psychedelic studies](https://allsci.com/news/approvals/fda-points-way-forward-for-compass-pathways-psilocybin-in-treatment-resistant-depression/)
— methodological challenges the agency noted are not unique to MDMA.

**Conduct concerns.** The application also drew scrutiny over trial conduct and the handling of
adverse events, and over the difficulty of separating the drug's effect from the accompanying
psychotherapy — which was not itself a regulated component.

**In July 2026 the FDA finalized guidance** for psychedelic trials specifically addressing
functional unblinding. That guidance exists substantially because of what happened here.

**Where the evidence stands.** Phase 3 trials reported large effects on PTSD symptoms. The regulator
concluded those results could not be adequately interpreted given the methodological problems, and
required another trial. Both statements are true, and the second is not a judgment that the
treatment does not work.

## What this means for how you read any of this

Here is why we have given the rejection this much space in a wellness publication.

**The problem that stopped MDMA is the problem running through almost everything on this site.**
You cannot blind someone to acupuncture, a sound bath, a float tank, a massage, a yoga class or a
retreat. Expectancy contaminates the result, and the more dramatic the experience, the worse the
contamination.

Our [journal](/journal/reiki-two-reviews) documents this repeatedly. What is different here is
that the FDA wrote a guidance document about it and refused an application over it. Most of the
wellness industry has never been asked the question.

When you read that a practice produces large effects, the question worth asking is: **could the
participants tell what they were getting?** If they could, the effect includes their expectation of
it — and that is true whether the intervention is a Schedule I drug or a scented oil.

## What it does not do

**It is not available as treatment in the US** as of the date above.

**It is not a substitute for the treatments that work now.**
[Prolonged exposure](/clinic/prolonged-exposure-ptsd), [cognitive processing
therapy](/clinic/cognitive-processing-therapy) and [EMDR](/clinic/emdr-ptsd-treatment) all carry
strong recommendations in the 2023 VA/DoD guideline, are available today, and are covered.

**It is not risk-free.** MDMA raises heart rate and blood pressure, carries cardiovascular risk,
and can cause hyperthermia. Psychiatric risks include acute distress during sessions and
post-session mood disturbance. Trials screen out significant cardiac conditions and psychotic-
spectrum disorders.

**And the therapy component is not incidental.** Reports of boundary violations during MDMA-assisted
sessions have been part of the field's regulatory difficulty. A drug that increases trust and
suggestibility, administered over eight hours by two people to someone with a trauma history, is a
situation requiring serious safeguards — which is exactly what an unregulated setting does not
have.

## What to do if you have PTSD now

**Start with the treatments that have guideline recommendations and are available.** The 2023
VA/DoD guideline strongly recommends CPT, prolonged exposure and EMDR, and — a point worth knowing
— recommends **psychotherapy over medication** for PTSD.

Retention differs between them in ways that matter: PE loses more people, and dropout spikes after
the first imaginal exposure session, while CPT retains better. Our articles on
[PE](/clinic/prolonged-exposure-ptsd) and [CPT](/clinic/cognitive-processing-therapy) cover how to
choose, and [written exposure therapy](/clinic/written-exposure-therapy) is a five-session option
with a 6.3% dropout rate.

**If you want to access MDMA legally:** clinical trials, searchable on ClinicalTrials.gov, and FDA
Expanded Access in limited circumstances.

**If you are considering an underground provider:** no screening for cardiac or psychiatric
contraindications, no regulation of who is in the room with you for eight hours while you are
drug-affected and vulnerable, no adverse-event reporting, and no recourse. The concerns that
contributed to the FDA's decision were partly about exactly this category of risk in a *supervised
trial* setting.

## When this isn't enough

If first-line trauma treatments have failed you, that is a real situation and there are further
options — different protocols, combination approaches, specialist trauma services, and trial
participation.

**If you are in crisis or having thoughts of harming yourself**, contact your doctor, a crisis
line, or emergency services today. If you are a veteran in the US, the Veterans Crisis Line is
available 24 hours.
