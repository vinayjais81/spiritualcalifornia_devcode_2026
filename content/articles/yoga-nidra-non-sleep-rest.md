In a Delhi sleep laboratory, between 2012 and 2016, forty-one people with chronic
insomnia were randomized into two groups. One received cognitive behavioural therapy for
insomnia -- the treatment every clinical guideline names first, ahead of medication. The
other was taught yoga nidra, a guided practice in which you lie still and are talked
through your own body while doing nothing whatsoever.

Both groups then slept with electrodes on their scalps.

## What it actually is

Yoga nidra means yogic sleep, which is a slightly misleading translation. You lie on your
back in *shavasana*, and a teacher or a recording moves your attention systematically
around the body -- right thumb, second finger, third finger, and onward through a fixed
sequence -- often followed by breath awareness, a rotation through opposite sensations,
and a brief intention. Sessions run twenty to forty minutes. Newer secular repackagings
market a shortened version under the name non-sleep deep rest.

The instruction that distinguishes it from meditation is that you are asked to remain on
the threshold: aware, but not effortfully so, and not asleep. Teachers are trained to
teach novices during the day, when they are most alert, precisely to keep them from
falling asleep during the practice.

What the practitioner is doing, functionally, is quite specific. You are not
concentrating, not observing thoughts, and not relaxing muscles deliberately. You are
following instructions while lying down.

## What the research shows

Take the Delhi trial first, because it is the best-designed study in this literature.
Published by Datta and colleagues in the *National Medical Journal of India*, it used
both sleep diaries and polysomnography, and it compared yoga nidra against an active
gold-standard comparator rather than a waiting list.

Both interventions improved subjective total sleep time, sleep efficiency, wake after
sleep onset and subjective sleep quality. Objectively, yoga nidra showed
[marked improvement in N2 and N3 as a proportion of total sleep time](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34825538/),
and salivary cortisol fell significantly after practice (*p* = 0.041). N3 is deep,
slow-wave sleep -- the stage people mean when they say restorative -- and an increase in
it is a more interesting finding than a questionnaire score.

A [2026 systematic review of randomized trials](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/27683605251390728)
gathered what else exists: six RCTs, 244 participants in total, across chronic insomnia,
hypertension, acute insomnia, frontline healthcare workers and athletes. Most reported
significant improvements in sleep onset latency, total sleep time and sleep efficiency.

There is early physiological work too. Sharpe and colleagues published sleep-lab
investigations of yoga nidra in the *Journal of Psychosomatic Research* in 2023, and a
[2025 randomized controlled trial in *Stress and Health*](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12080877/)
examined diurnal salivary cortisol alongside subjective wellbeing. An earlier study by
Moszeik and colleagues tested a brief eleven-minute version and found improvements in
estimated sleep quality and daytime sleepiness, though the authors were explicit that the
effects were small.

**Now the honest accounting.** That same research
group's 2023 literature review identified forty-five studies on yoga nidra -- of which
only thirteen used a randomized group comparison, and only five included an active
control group. Six RCTs with 244 people between them is not a foundation for confident
claims, however consistent their direction. A practice this popular should have a much
larger evidence base than it does.

## What it does not do

**It is not a treatment for insomnia in the sense that CBT-I is.** The Delhi trial
authors concluded yoga nidra can be used for chronic insomnia *after supervised practice
sessions*, and noted in the same breath that CBT-I remains the effective
non-pharmacological treatment. The practice performed respectably against the standard.
It did not displace it.

**"Non-sleep deep rest" is a marketing term, not a research category.** You will not find
NSDR as an indexed intervention with its own trial literature. What exists is research on
yoga nidra, and the newer branding borrows that credibility while typically shortening
the practice and dropping the supervision.

**It does not replace sleep.** The claim that twenty minutes of yoga nidra substitutes
for hours of sleep circulates widely and has no support in this literature. Nothing here
measured that, and nothing here suggests it.

**And it is not a substitute for a sleep workup.** Persistent insomnia has causes --
apnoea, medication, thyroid function, depression, alcohol -- and a guided relaxation
practice will not find any of them.

## What a session is like

You lie on your back on a mat or a bed, covered with a blanket, in a dimly lit room.
Nothing is required of your body. A voice begins moving your attention around: it will
ask you to bring awareness to the right thumb, then each finger in turn, then the palm,
the wrist, and onward -- through the whole body, in a fixed order, at a pace that feels
almost absurdly slow.

Most first-timers fall asleep, which teachers treat as fine but not the aim. What people
notice when they stay on the threshold is a peculiar heaviness, occasional loss of the
body's edges, and a distorted sense of time -- forty minutes reported as ten. Some people
find the passivity intolerable for the first few sessions.

Classes typically run $20-$40 in California, and much of the practice is genuinely
suited to free recordings, which is unusual among the modalities on this site. The Delhi
trial's structure is the argument for beginning with a teacher: supervised sessions
first, self-practice afterwards.

## Who tends to find it useful

People often seek yoga nidra for trouble sleeping, for the specific exhaustion that
does not respond to a weekend, and after finding seated meditation physically
uncomfortable or mentally aggravating. It asks for no posture, no concentration and no
belief, which makes it one of the more accessible practices on this list -- including for
people who are unwell, in pain, or recovering.

It suits people who find instruction easier than autonomy. It suits people badly if
lying still with nothing to do reliably produces anxiety, which for some people it does.

## How to choose a teacher or recording

Yoga nidra is taught within yoga teacher training, so look for a credentialed yoga
teacher with specific yoga nidra training -- the Satyananda and Bihar School lineages
are the established sources for the classical protocol. Ask which lineage they teach
from and how long their sessions run.

For recordings, prefer a full-length classical sequence from a named teacher over a
ten-minute app track described as NSDR. The evidence above tested twenty to forty
minutes of structured body rotation, not a short relaxation.

Anyone claiming a session replaces sleep, or that the practice treats a diagnosed
condition, has left the evidence behind.
