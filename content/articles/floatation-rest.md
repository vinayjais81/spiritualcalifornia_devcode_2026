Floatation has a naming problem that has shaped how it is perceived. "Sensory deprivation tank"
evokes 1960s experiments and a Ken Russell film. The researchers studying it now use a different
term -- **Reduced Environmental Stimulation Therapy**, or floatation-REST -- and the reframing is
accurate rather than cosmetic.

The intervention is not deprivation. It is the removal of almost every external signal
competing for your attention, which turns out to be a rather different proposition.

## What it actually is

You float on your back in about ten inches of water saturated with roughly 800 to 1,000 pounds
of Epsom salt, making the water dense enough that you cannot sink. The water is heated to skin
temperature, so the boundary between body and water becomes difficult to locate. The tank or
pool room is dark and soundproofed.

The research description is precise: the experience is calibrated so that visual, auditory,
olfactory, gustatory, thermal, tactile, vestibular, gravitational and proprioceptive signals are
all minimized, along with most movement and speech.

Sessions run 45 to 90 minutes. Modern float rooms are open pools rather than coffin-like tanks,
which matters for anyone claustrophobic.

The proposed mechanism is interoceptive: with external input stripped away, attention turns
inward toward bodily sensation, and the profound reduction in vestibular and proprioceptive
signalling appears to produce deep parasympathetic relaxation.

## What the research shows

The serious research programme is centred at the Laureate Institute for Brain Research, and its
work is unusually well designed for this field.

**Single sessions produce measurable acute effects.** Feinstein and colleagues found that single
sessions of floatation-REST in anxious and depressed individuals were safe, well tolerated, and
associated with an
[acute anxiolytic and antidepressant effect persisting for over 48 hours](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0286899).
Related work examined relaxation and interoceptive awareness in people with high anxiety
sensitivity.

**A pilot trial in generalized anxiety disorder** randomized 50 participants to twelve sessions
or a waitlist and reported a significant reduction in generalized anxiety symptoms in the float
group, maintained at six-month follow-up. Waitlist comparison, small sample -- promising rather
than conclusive.

**The best-designed trial is about feasibility, not efficacy, and that is worth understanding.**
Garland and colleagues randomized 75 individuals with anxiety and depression -- screened from
1,715 -- to six sessions in one of three arms: weekly one-hour floats, floats with flexible
duration and frequency, or an
[active comparator of weekly one-hour sessions in a zero-gravity chair](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11156321/).

That chair comparator deserves credit. It controls for the hour, the darkness, the quiet, the
lying still and the trip to the clinic -- everything except the water. Very little in this
journal has a comparator that fair.

The primary outcome was adherence: 85% for the fixed float condition, 89% for the flexible one,
74% for the chair. Dropout did not differ significantly between conditions. So: anxious and
depressed people will reliably attend repeated float sessions, and they attend somewhat more
readily than they attend a chair.

That is a genuinely useful finding, and it is not a finding that floating outperforms the chair
on symptoms.

**Where the evidence stands.** Consistent acute effects on anxiety from small studies, one waitlist-controlled
pilot, and a well-designed active-controlled trial that established feasibility rather than
efficacy. A health technology review has assessed the evidence for mental health conditions
using formal quality appraisal, and the picture is the same: early.

## What it does not do

**It has not been shown to treat anxiety or depression.** Acute effects lasting 48 hours after a
single session are real and are not a treatment. The efficacy trial with an active comparator
has not yet been run at scale.

**It does not induce a special brain state that has been characterized.** Claims about theta
wave states and enhanced creativity are extrapolations, not findings.

**Magnesium absorption through the skin is not established.** Epsom salt is there to make you
float. The idea that you absorb therapeutic magnesium through the skin during a float lacks
good evidence.

**And the reported hallucinations are worth naming honestly.** Auditory and visual hallucinations
during floatation-REST are documented. They are infrequent and generally described positively by
those experiencing them -- but if you have a psychosis history, this is a conversation to have
with a clinician first.

**Safety, and this is the part float centres rarely mention.** The research literature notes that
several deaths have been reported at recreational float centres, associated with concurrent
drug -- specifically ketamine -- or alcohol use, and argues for substance use screening as a
consequence. You are alone, in the dark, in water, with no monitoring. Do not float impaired.

Minor documented effects include skin itchiness, dry mouth, and discomfort from saltwater
entering the eyes or open cuts. Do not float with fresh cuts or after shaving.

## What a session is like

You shower, insert earplugs, and enter a dark pool room or tank. The water is shallow and
skin-temperature, and the first surprise is how completely the salt holds you up -- no effort at
all is required.

The first ten to fifteen minutes are usually restless. People adjust their arms, notice their
heartbeat, and find the darkness more present than expected. Then, for most, the sense of the
body's edges begins to blur, and it becomes difficult to say where the water stops.

Time perception distorts substantially. Sixty minutes commonly reads as twenty or as
considerably longer. Some people sleep. Some experience vivid imagery. A minority find it
unpleasant -- claustrophobic, or too much unstructured internal space -- and that is worth
knowing before booking a package.

Afterwards there is a characteristic slowed, softened quality that lasts a few hours, and many
people report the best sleep of the week.

Expect $60-$100 per session in the Bay Area, with packages cheaper. Sessions are typically 60
or 90 minutes.

## Who tends to find it useful

People often come for anxiety and for sleep, and the acute anxiolytic research supports that
use. It also draws people who cannot meditate but want the same thing -- and floating removes
almost every distraction that makes sitting still difficult, which is arguably the most honest
description of what you are buying.

It suits people comfortable with darkness, enclosed spaces and their own company for an hour.
It suits poorly anyone claustrophobic (though open float rooms help), anyone with a psychosis
history without medical advice, and anyone who cannot resist trying to *use* the time
productively.

Our honest framing: this is an expensive hour of extremely effective distraction removal, with
promising acute evidence for anxiety and no demonstrated advantage yet over lying quietly in a
comfortable chair in the dark. That comparison exists, it was well designed, and it measured
attendance rather than symptoms. Until someone runs the efficacy version, the honest answer is
that we do not know.

## How to choose a facility

**Ask about water treatment.** Filtration between every float, UV or ozone treatment, and
regular water testing. The salt concentration is inhospitable to most organisms but the water is
shared, and standards vary.

**Ask about open pools versus enclosed tanks** if enclosure concerns you. Most modern facilities
offer both.

**Ask about screening and monitoring.** Given the documented deaths involving substances, a
facility that asks whether you have been drinking or using drugs is one taking the single real
risk seriously.

**Ask about controls.** You should be able to turn on a light, open the door, and communicate.
Knowing that usually removes the need to use it.

**And listen to the claims.** "Most people find this deeply relaxing and it may reduce anxiety" is
supported. Magnesium absorption, detoxification, brainwave optimization and creativity
enhancement are not.
