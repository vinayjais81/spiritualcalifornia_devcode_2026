# Spiritual California -- The 50-Article Journal Plan

Built around the modalities actually offered on the site, then extended into the
adjacent topics your audience searches for. Ordered into seven clusters so the journal
index has real navigation rather than a reverse-chronological pile.

**Status: all 50 written.** Two tiers were revised downward during drafting and are marked ~~struck~~ below.

**How to read the columns**

- **Tier** = the evidence tier the article will land in, forecast from the current
  literature. It sets the language before a word is written. A forecast tier can be
  revised downward once the sources are read -- never upward to be more flattering.
- **Evidence anchor** = the specific paper or body of work the article is built on. This
  is what makes each piece a two-hour job instead of a two-day one.
- Image filename is always `{slug}.webp`. Prompt numbers match `03-IMAGE-PROMPTS-50.md`.

Legend: **A** established · **B** some trials, modest/mixed · **C** preliminary · **D** traditional, unsupported

## Publishing cadence

50 articles at two per week is a six-month runway. Publish in cluster order rather
than by traffic potential -- the clusters build a coherent library, and internal links
between neighbours in the same cluster do more for search than chasing keywords.

**Batch order for writing:** finish Cluster 1 and 2 first. They carry the strongest
evidence and the highest search volume, so they establish the journal's authority
before the Tier C and D pieces arrive.

## A note on the Tier D articles

Pieces 20, 21, 30 and 48 have no supporting clinical trials, and they say so in the
second paragraph. Keep them. An article that openly reports "there are no trials here,
and here is what we do know about why people find it valuable anyway" is more
persuasive to your audience than any article claiming evidence it does not have. They
are also the pages most likely to be linked to by other sites, precisely because
almost nobody else writes them honestly.

If the marketplace later adds astrology, tarot or numerology practitioners, those
articles follow the same Tier D treatment: describe the practice accurately, state
plainly that predictive validity is not supported, and cover what the research on
narrative, ritual and structured self-reflection does show.
