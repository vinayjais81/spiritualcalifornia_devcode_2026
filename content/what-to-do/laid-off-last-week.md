The thing nobody warns you about is not the money or the pride. It is that every external
structure disappeared on the same day — when you got up, where you went, who expected you,
what the week was shaped like.

Motivation does not survive that on its own. Do not wait for it.

## Do this now

This is behavioural activation, in its simplest form. The instruction that makes it work is
**action first, feeling later** — you do the thing regardless of whether you want to.

1. **Fix a wake time and keep it.** Every day, including weekends. This is the single load-bearing
   decision of the next month.
2. **Get outside within an hour of waking**, for fifteen minutes. Job loss disrupts sleep timing
   fast, and morning daylight is the strongest available correction.
3. **Schedule three specific things a day.** Specific means *Tuesday 10am, update CV for one
   hour* — not "work on job search." Deliberately small.
4. **Make at least one of the three not about the job.** A walk, a coffee with someone, an errand.
   All three being job-search collapses your whole day into the thing you have lost.
5. **Do them whether or not you feel like it.** This is not motivational advice. Waiting to feel
   ready is the trap the treatment is designed to bypass.
6. **Tell one person what you scheduled.** Accountability does more than intention.

## If that doesn't fit

**If three things is too many:** do one. The number matters far less than the fact that it is
written down with a time attached.

**If you cannot face anyone yet:** that is common in the first fortnight and usually passes. Keep
the wake time and the daylight even in the weeks where the rest does not happen.

## Why structure before meaning

Behavioural activation works from the outside in. Depression — and job loss reliably produces
something that looks like it — narrows behaviour; the narrowing reduces reward and contact; the
reduction deepens the low mood. The loop is broken at the behaviour, not at the belief.

The evidence is unusually strong for something this simple. The **COBRA trial** randomized 440
adults with major depression to either behavioural activation delivered by *junior mental health
workers* or CBT delivered by trained psychological therapists.

At twelve months, both groups finished at
[**8.4 points on the PHQ-9 — a mean difference of 0.1
points**](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27461440/). Around two-thirds of both groups achieved at
least a 50% reduction in symptoms.

A simpler treatment, delivered by less specialised staff, matched the gold standard.

**What this means for you practically:** the scheduling *is* the intervention. It is not
preparation for feeling better. And you do not need a therapist to start it — behavioural
activation is one of the few treatments in this library where guided self-help is a legitimate
route rather than a compromise, which our article on
[guided self-help](/clinic/guided-self-help) covers.

**Meaning comes later, and it does come.** The question of what you actually want to do next is
real and worth answering — but it answers badly in week two, from a standing start, with no
structure and no sleep. Set that aside deliberately for now. Our article on
[being burnt out](/what-to-do/burnt-out-holiday-didnt-fix-it) covers the values question when you
are ready for it.

## The practical layer, which is also mental health

Some of what feels like despair is unresolved logistics. Doing these early removes a
disproportionate amount of background dread:

- **File for unemployment immediately.** In California this is EDD, and there is no reason to
  wait — benefits are generally backdated to when you file, not to when you lost the job.
- **Sort health coverage.** COBRA is expensive; Covered California is often much cheaper, and job
  loss opens a special enrolment period. Do not let this drift.
- **Work out your actual runway** — a number, on paper. Vague financial fear is worse than a
  specific one, and it costs an hour to replace.
- **Tell more people than feels comfortable.** Most roles come through weak ties, and this is also
  the fastest antidote to the isolation that follows job loss.

## When this isn't enough

Job loss is a genuine grief and a recognised risk factor for depression. Feeling flattened for a
few weeks is expected.

**See a doctor if**, after several weeks: sleep or appetite has substantially changed, you have
stopped enjoying anything at all, you are drinking more, you cannot get out of bed most days, or
the low mood is constant rather than fluctuating.

**And if you are having thoughts of harming yourself, contact your doctor, a crisis line, or
emergency services today.** Job loss is a well-documented risk period, particularly for men in
midlife. That is not a reason for alarm about yourself — it is a reason to treat this as a time to
have support around you rather than to get through it alone.
