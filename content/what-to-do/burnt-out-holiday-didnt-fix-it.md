The holiday worked while you were on it. Then you came back, and by Thursday it was as though it
had not happened.

That is not a failure of the holiday. It is information: **rest treats tiredness, and what you
have is probably not tiredness.**

## Do this now

There is no ninety-second technique on this page, because the problem does not have one. There is
a question, and it takes about twenty minutes to answer honestly.

1. **Write down how you actually spent last week.** Not the calendar — the hours. Work, commuting,
   admin, screens, obligations, sleep.
2. **Next to each, mark whether it connects to something you would say matters to you.** Not
   whether it was necessary. Whether it *matters*.
3. **Look at the proportion.** Most people who are burnt out find the marked column almost empty,
   and are surprised by it rather than reassured.
4. **Then, one sentence:** what is one thing on that list you could do less of, and one thing not
   on it you could add this month?
5. **Make the addition involve other people**, if you can. See below for why.

That is the whole exercise. It is deliberately not restful.

## If that doesn't fit

**If the honest answer is "I cannot change anything":** that may be true for now, and the useful
next step is a conversation rather than a worksheet — with a manager, a partner, or a therapist.

**If you cannot face the audit:** the smaller version is to name one thing you used to do that you
have stopped doing, and do it once this week.

## Why rest didn't work

Researchers followed **444 people walking the Camino de Santiago** and compared them against
**124 people taking ordinary non-pilgrimage holidays**. That comparator matters — it controls for
time off, travel, novelty and rest.

The pilgrims did better on positive affect, life satisfaction, anxiety, depression and perceived
stress. Then the researchers tested *what the benefit travelled through*, and the answer was
**valued living** — the degree to which daily actions align with what a person considers
important.

Valued living
[**fully mediated** the effects on perceived stress, negative affect, life satisfaction and
subjective happiness](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/353121429_The_effects_of_the_pilgrimage_to_Santiago_de_Compostela_Way_of_Saint_James_on_mental_health_and_wellbeing).

Fully mediated means the advantage over an ordinary holiday was explained by the change in valued
living. Not by the exercise. Not by the scenery. Not by the rest.

**That is the finding to sit with.** If what has depleted you is spending your days on things that
do not connect to anything you care about, then a week of not doing those things is a pause, not
a repair.

We should be honest about the study's limits: you cannot randomize people to a pilgrimage, the
participants self-selected, and the measures are self-reported. But the direction is consistent
with a separate and much larger literature — the same construct, **values-based action**, is what
[acceptance and commitment therapy](/clinic/acceptance-and-commitment-therapy) is most confident
about moving.

**And the connection point.** Across this journal, being reliably in a room with the same people
recurs as a mechanism more often than almost anything else — in [group
drumming](/journal/group-drumming-cortisol), [group therapy](/clinic/group-therapy), and the
[ikigai research](/journal/ikigai-without-the-infographic), where the mortality pattern points
toward social and cardiovascular rather than cellular causes. If your addition can involve other
people, make it so.

## When this isn't enough

**Burnout and depression overlap and are not the same thing.** Burnout is classically
work-specific — exhaustion, cynicism about the job, reduced sense of effectiveness — and lifts, at
least somewhat, when you are away from it.

If the flatness follows you everywhere, if you have stopped enjoying things unrelated to work, if
sleep or appetite has changed, or if you are having thoughts of harming yourself, that is
depression rather than burnout and it needs a GP rather than a values audit. It responds well to
treatment — [several of them](/clinic/behavioural-activation).

**Two other things worth ruling out**, because they present exactly like this and are easily
tested: thyroid dysfunction and anaemia.

**And if the honest conclusion is that the job is wrong**, that is a decision rather than a
symptom. Our article on [life coaching](/journal/life-coaching-evidence) covers what structured
help with that kind of decision does and does not deliver — including the finding that more
sessions did not predict better outcomes.
