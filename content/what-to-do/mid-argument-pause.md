You can hear it queuing up. The sentence that is accurate, devastating, and will still be in the
room in six months.

## Do this now

**Say this, out loud, in these words:**

> *"I need twenty minutes. I'm not walking out — I'll come back at [specific time] and we'll
> finish this."*

Then:

1. **Go to a different room, or outside.** Not the same space.
2. **Breathe out longer than you breathe in.** Four in, six out, for five minutes.
3. **Do not use the twenty minutes to build your case.** Rehearsing is the opposite of what the
   break is for, and you will come back more armed than when you left.
4. **Come back when you said you would.** This is the part that matters most.
5. **Open with what you want**, not with what they did. *"I want us to be able to talk about money
   without this happening"* lands differently from *"you always..."*.

## If that doesn't fit

**If you cannot leave the room** — a car, a restaurant, other people present — the smaller version
is: stop talking, put your feet flat on the floor, and let the silence sit for ten seconds. Ten
seconds is longer than it sounds and it interrupts the momentum.

**If they follow you:** *"I'm coming back at [time]. I need the twenty minutes so I don't say
something I can't take back."* Naming the reason usually works better than repeating the request.

## Why the stated return matters

**Walking out and stating a pause are experienced as completely different things** by the person
you leave.

This is the pursue–withdraw cycle that [emotionally focused couple
therapy](/clinic/emotionally-focused-couple-therapy) is built around. One partner escalates to get
a response; the other withdraws to stop the escalation; the withdrawal reads as abandonment and
raises the stakes; the escalation reads as attack and drives further withdrawal. Neither person is
choosing it and both are certain the other started it.

An unexplained exit is the withdrawal move, and it feeds the loop. **A stated pause with a return
time is not.** It communicates the opposite of what withdrawal communicates: *I am coming back.*

**On the breathing**, slowing to around six cycles a minute with a longer exhale raises
parasympathetic control of the heart
([Laborde et al., 2022](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0149763422002007);
[Bae et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1111/psyp.13905)). Five minutes is as effective as twenty.
The effect is modest — enough to lower the volume, not enough to change your mind for you.

**Twenty minutes is roughly what physiological arousal needs to come down**, which is why the
number in the script is not arbitrary. Coming back at five is coming back mid-argument with a
short interruption.

## When this isn't enough

If the same argument recurs in the same shape, the content is not the problem — the cycle is. That
is precisely what [couples therapy](/clinic/emotionally-focused-couple-therapy) works on, and it
is more tractable than most people expect.

**One firm line.** If there is any violence, intimidation, or fear in your relationship, none of
this applies and couples therapy is not the right first step — conjoint work in the presence of
abuse can increase risk. Contact a domestic violence service or your doctor. In the US, the
National Domestic Violence Hotline operates 24 hours.

And if you are the one whose anger frightens you, that is worth a conversation with a GP or
therapist rather than a better pausing technique.
