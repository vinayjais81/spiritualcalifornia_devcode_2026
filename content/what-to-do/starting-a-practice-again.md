You have tried this before. You downloaded the app, sat down with genuine intent, managed nine
days, and stopped. Possibly more than once.

Here is the finding that changes the plan: across randomized trials of mindfulness programmes,
**how much you practise did not reliably predict how much good it did.**

## Do this now

1. **Ten minutes, not forty-five.** This is not a compromise. See below.
2. **Attach it to something you already do without deciding.** After the shower. Before the first
   email. In the parked car before you walk into the building. Do not schedule it in isolation,
   where it competes with everything.
3. **Use a structured course, not a library of one-off sessions.** An eight-week programme with
   teaching, in order. This matters more than the length.
4. **Expect the second and third week to be bad.** The mind is loud, ten minutes feels long, and
   nothing appears to be happening. This is the standard experience, not a sign it isn't working.
5. **Count returns, not calm.** The practice is noticing you have drifted and coming back. If that
   happened forty times, you did it correctly forty times.
6. **If sitting is the obstacle, walk instead.** Mindful movement is a component of the original
   clinical programme, not a concession.

## If that doesn't fit

**If ten minutes is still too much:** start at five. The dose evidence does not punish you for it.

**If silence makes you anxious:** try a guided body-scan or yoga nidra recording instead. It asks
nothing of you and requires no concentration.

**If you have tried repeatedly and hated every minute:** consider that seated meditation may
simply not be your practice. Tai chi, walking meditation, chanting and group music-making all
occupy attention differently, and all have their own evidence.

## Why it works

The paper worth knowing is a dose-response meta-regression by Strohmaier, published in
*Mindfulness*, which pooled randomized trials of mindfulness-based programmes and asked whether
more delivered more. Programme length, teacher contact and home-practice volume were
[**not significantly associated** with psychological
outcomes](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12671-020-01319-4).

That has held up in direct tests. A 2023 study randomized participants to ten versus twenty
minutes of daily practice
([*Scientific Reports*](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-023-46578-y)), and researchers
reviewing the field note that most trials deliberately manipulating session length have failed to
detect a dose-response effect at all.

The underlying practice is not weak. Mindfulness-based interventions produce moderate-to-large
effects against no-treatment controls. The effect simply does not appear to scale with minutes
the way a training effect would.

**What did predict outcomes.** The same review found that programmes *lacking a didactic
component* — teaching, explanation, discussion — produced smaller effects. **What** you are doing
appears to matter more than **how long** you do it. That is the reason for point 3 above: an
unguided ten minutes is not the same intervention as a taught ten minutes.

**One honest limit.** This does not mean any practice is as good as any other, and it does not
mean the traditional claim is wrong. The contemplative traditions describe something that emerges
over years of long sitting, and no trial has measured that. What the research licenses is a
modest, achievable, evidence-consistent starting point — not a claim that ten minutes is the
whole of it.

## When this isn't enough

**One thing that matters more than the schedule:** if you have a history of trauma, dissociation
or psychosis, say so before you start, whatever the dose. A minority of people find intensive
practice destabilizing, and a competent teacher will ask about this. If yours does not, that
tells you something.

If you are trying to start a practice because you are anxious or low rather than because you are
curious, be aware of what the serious version costs. In the trial where an eight-week mindfulness
course held its own against a first-line antidepressant, the dose was forty-five minutes daily
plus a weekly class plus a full-day retreat — roughly forty hours over eight weeks
([Hoge et al., *JAMA Psychiatry*,
2023](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamapsychiatry/fullarticle/2798510)). Ten minutes a day is
a good habit. It is not the intervention that produced those results, and it should not be sold
to you as though it were.

If what you are dealing with is a diagnosable condition, treat this as an addition to care rather
than a substitute for it.
