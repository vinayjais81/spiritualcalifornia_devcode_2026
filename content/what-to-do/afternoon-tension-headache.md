It arrives around four, sits behind the eyes or across the forehead like a band, and you have
started treating it as part of the job.

## First: headaches that are not this

Most recurrent afternoon headaches in desk workers are tension-type. **Some headaches need urgent
assessment**, and these are the ones:

- **Sudden, severe, "worst headache of my life"** — reaching maximum intensity within seconds to a
  minute. This is a thunderclap headache and it is an emergency.
- **Headache with fever and a stiff neck**, or with a rash
- **Headache after a head injury**
- **New neurological symptoms** — weakness, numbness, difficulty speaking, visual loss, confusion
- **Headache that wakes you from sleep**, or is consistently worst on waking and improves through
  the day
- **Headache made distinctly worse by coughing, straining or lying flat**
- **A new headache pattern starting after age 50**
- **A headache that is progressively worsening** week on week

Any of those is a call to a doctor, and the first four are urgent today. Everything below assumes
none apply.

## Do this now

Four things, in order, before you reach for anything else. This takes ten minutes.

1. **Drink a full glass of water.** Read the next section before dismissing this one.
2. **Go outside for five minutes.** Not the window — outside.
3. **Look at something more than twenty feet away for twenty seconds**, then repeat a few times.
   Your eyes have been at fixed close focus since morning.
4. **Check your jaw and shoulders**, and let both drop. Tension-type headache is frequently
   referred from the neck and jaw.

**Then, for tomorrow:**

5. **Front-load water in the morning**, not at 4pm.
6. **Raise your screen** so the top of it is at eye level, and move it an arm's length away.
7. **Log it for two weeks** — time of onset, what you had eaten and drunk, sleep the night before,
   what you were doing. Patterns emerge that no single afternoon reveals.

## Why start with water

Fatigue, difficulty concentrating, headache and low mood are precisely the symptom cluster that
mild dehydration produces in controlled trials — at losses of 1 to 2% of body mass, which most
people reach across a busy day without noticing
([Ganio et al., *British Journal of Nutrition*, 2011](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/british-journal-of-nutrition/article/mild-dehydration-impairs-cognitive-performance-and-mood-of-men/3388AB36B8DF73E844C9AD19271A75BF)).

The threshold is lower than most people assume: one analysis found that drinking water after a
loss of just **0.72% of body mass** prevented declines in memory, attention and mood
([Benton et al., 2016](https://ajcn.nutrition.org/article/S0002-9165(22)04582-8/fulltext)).

**One disclosure we make wherever we cite this**, because we make it elsewhere in this library:
several of the key hydration studies include authors affiliated with a bottled water company's
research division. The trials themselves are randomized and controlled, and the finding is
independently plausible — but you should know who has an interest in it.

**On the daylight and distance vision**, neither has trial evidence for headache specifically.
What is established is that a day spent entirely at 300-500 lux indoor lighting is an impoverished
light environment compared with the roughly 10,000 lux of an overcast morning outdoors, and that
sustained close focus is a plausible contributor to eye strain. We are labelling those as
reasonable rather than proven.

## The one to be careful about

**Medication-overuse headache is real and easy to fall into.** Taking painkillers for headache on
more than about ten to fifteen days a month can, over time, produce a headache pattern caused by
the treatment.

If you are taking something most afternoons, that is worth raising with a doctor rather than
continuing. Coming off requires a plan and is usually uncomfortable, which is why it is worth
catching early.

## When this isn't enough

**See a doctor** if headaches occur most days, if over-the-counter medication has stopped working,
if the pattern has changed, or if you have started organising your life around them.

Worth having checked: **blood pressure**, **vision** — an eye test is cheap and uncorrected
refractive error is a common and entirely fixable cause — and **sleep apnoea** if you also wake
unrefreshed, which our article on
[sleeping eight hours and feeling terrible](/what-to-do/slept-eight-hours-still-tired) covers.

If the headaches are one-sided, throbbing, accompanied by nausea or light sensitivity, or preceded
by visual disturbance, that is migraine rather than tension-type — and migraine has specific
effective treatments that tension-type headache advice will not deliver. It is worth naming
accurately to a doctor.
